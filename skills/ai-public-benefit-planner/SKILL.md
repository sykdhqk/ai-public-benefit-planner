# AI Public Benefit Planner

Turn an AI-for-good idea into a decision document that reviewers can challenge, measure, and govern. Keep facts, assumptions, and proposed commitments visibly separate.

## Inputs

Collect the initiative, intended beneficiaries, geography, delivery partners, available budget or resources, time horizon, and evidence already known. Ask only for missing details that materially change the plan. Mark unresolved facts as `Unknown`; do not invent numbers, partners, consent, or legal authority.

Read [references/planning-rules.md](references/planning-rules.md) before drafting. Use [references/plan-template.md](references/plan-template.md) for the final structure.

## Workflow

1. Restate the proposed public benefit in one falsifiable outcome sentence.
2. Map primary beneficiaries, excluded or burdened groups, operators, funders, and accountable decision makers.
3. Build a theory of change from resources to activities, outputs, near-term outcomes, and public benefit. Mark every unsupported causal link as an assumption.
4. Define a small metric set with baseline, target, data source, cadence, owner, and anti-gaming check. Use `Unknown` where evidence is absent.
5. Specify governance: decision rights, beneficiary participation, complaints, conflicts of interest, publication, privacy, safety, and independent review.
6. Add funding safeguards: restricted uses, milestone releases, procurement transparency, related-party disclosure, audit evidence, and remaining-funds treatment.
7. Define pilot gates, pause triggers, stop conditions, and a responsible exit path.
8. End with the smallest evidence requests that could change the decision.

## Boundaries

- Do not claim social impact from activity counts alone.
- Do not convert assumptions into facts or fabricate baselines and targets.
- Do not infer that nonprofit status, charitable intent, or a public-benefit label proves benefit.
- Do not provide legal, tax, investment, medical, or regulatory determinations; identify where qualified review is required.
- Do not recommend collecting sensitive data unless it is necessary, proportionate, consented, and governed.
- Prefer a reversible pilot when causal evidence, distributional effects, or operational capacity is uncertain.

## Output

Produce the plan in the template order. Each metric must have an owner and evidence source. Each material risk must have a trigger, response, and accountable role. Finish with a decision of `Proceed to pilot`, `Revise`, or `Do not proceed`, followed by the evidence needed to revisit it.
